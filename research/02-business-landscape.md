# Business Landscape

# Who SV should pursue

SV should choose customers by **economic shape**, not simply by industry.

## Five target profiles

### 01 Complex Operator
Multi-site, multi-country, system-heavy businesses. Best entry points: order-to-cash, supply chain exceptions, collections, service and planning.

### 02 Growth-Constrained Business
Demand exists but revenue leaks between intent, qualification, proposal, conversion and expansion.

### 03 Scale-Constrained Business
The business works, but growth adds people and management complexity almost linearly.

### 04 Transformation-Ready Enterprise
AI pilots exist, but systems, data, governance and workflows remain fragmented.

### 05 New Business / New Venture
A new product, geography or channel can be designed as an intelligent system from day one.

## Qualification KPIs

Score each account on a 100-point internal fit model:

- Value potential 25
- Workflow complexity 15
- System fragmentation 15
- Data/context availability 10
- AI readiness 10
- Executive urgency 10
- Pilotability within 90 days 10
- Expansion potential 5

## CXO stories

**CEO / Owner:** “How do I grow without multiplying complexity?”

**COO:** “Where is operational friction costing us speed, money or reliability?”

**CRO:** “Where are we losing revenue between intent and cash?”

**CIO / CTO:** “How do I turn AI pilots into a coherent enterprise capability?”

## Example account pattern

Imagine a packaging group operating across the UK, China and India. It has ERP, CRM, supply chain planning, email, documents and human coordination. The opportunity is not to replace ERP. It is to connect signals and decisions across the value chain so routine work can move automatically and humans handle exceptions.
